Key Principles
  • Anode: Site of Oxidation (Loss of e⁻). Mass decreases.
  • Cathode: Site of Reduction (Gain of e⁻). Mass increases.
  • Electrons: Flow from Anode to Cathode in wire.
  • Salt Bridge: Maintains electrical neutrality (Anions → Anode beaker, Cations → Cathode beaker).

How to Use the Electrochemical Simulator

Master galvanic cells, redox reactions, and the Nernst equation

1. Selecting Metals: Choose metals for the left and right electrodes. The metal with the lower standard reduction potential ($E^\circ$) will naturally act as the Anode (oxidation), while the higher $E^\circ$ metal becomes the Cathode (reduction).

2. Circuit Switch & Salt Bridge:
• Disconnecting the switch stops electron flow in the wire.
• Removing the salt bridge prevents ion migration, building up charge imbalance and stopping the current immediately.

3. Concentration & Nernst Equation: Use the sliders to adjust electrolyte ion concentrations ($[M^{n+}]$). Non-standard concentrations change the reaction quotient $Q$ and shift the cell potential according to: $$\text{E}_{\text{cell}} = \text{E}^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln Q$$

4. Microscopic View: Click "Show Atomic Inset" to zoom into the electrode-solution interface and watch metal atoms lose electrons into the wire or aqueous ions gain electrons and deposit onto the electrode surface.
